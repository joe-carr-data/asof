# Part II — Heuristics and Biases

Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow*, chapters 10–18.

## The triad of System 1 heuristics

1. **Anchoring** — even arbitrary numbers shown before an estimate pull the estimate toward themselves. Spinning a wheel that lands on 65 makes people guess Africa is "65% of UN members" higher than if it landed on 10.
2. **Availability** — likelihood judgments default to "how easy is it to bring an example to mind?" rather than actual base rates. Recent vivid cases dominate.
3. **Representativeness** — "Linda the bank teller" — people judge probability by similarity to a stereotype, not by base-rate logic. Produces conjunction-fallacy errors.

## Why these matter

System 1 is not stupid; the heuristics work most of the time. The point isn't to reject them but to know when to engage System 2 anyway. Kahneman's recurring advice: **slow down for high-stakes decisions** even when System 1 says everything's fine.
